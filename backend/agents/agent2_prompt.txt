CURATED KNOWLEDGE BASE — BACTERIAL ENZYME ORTHOLOGS
Use ONLY the following data. Do not hallucinate bacterial capabilities.
BACTEROIDETES PHYLUM
Bacteroides fragilis

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2D6-like reductase (Tier 2, ~45% identity), CYP3A4-like oxidase (Tier 2, ~40% identity)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — strong producer (Tier 1, confirmed in literature)
Other drug-metabolizing enzymes: Nitroreductases, azoreductases
Neurotransmitter relevance: GABA production (Tier 2)
Key reference: Zimmermann et al. 2019, Nature; Spanogiannopoulos et al. 2016

Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2C19-like reductase (Tier 2, ~38% identity)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — moderate producer (Tier 1, confirmed)
Other: Broad glycoside hydrolase activity
Neurotransmitter relevance: SCFA production (butyrate precursors) (Tier 1)
Key reference: Pollet et al. 2017

Bacteroides vulgatus

CYP450 orthologs: CYP3A4-like activity (Tier 3, predicted from chemical class analogy)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — moderate producer (Tier 2, inferred)
Neurotransmitter relevance: Tryptophan metabolism — shifts toward kynurenine pathway (Tier 2)
Key reference: Guo et al. 2019

Prevotella copri

CYP450 orthologs: Limited evidence. CYP2C19-like activity (Tier 3, predicted)
β-glucuronidase: Low/minimal (Tier 3)
Neurotransmitter relevance: SCFA production (propionate) (Tier 1)
Key reference: Franke & Zeevi 2022

FIRMICUTES PHYLUM
Clostridium scindens

CYP450 orthologs: CYP3A4-like 7α-dehydroxylase (Tier 2, ~42% identity) — primarily bile acid metabolism but may act on drug substrates with similar steroid-like structures
β-glucuronidase: Yes — moderate producer (Tier 1, confirmed)
Other: Bile acid 7α-dehydroxylation (converts primary to secondary bile acids)
Neurotransmitter relevance: Indirect — bile acid signaling affects serotonin synthesis via FXR/TGR5 (Tier 2)
Key reference: Ridlon et al. 2014

Clostridium bolteae

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2D6-like activity (Tier 3, predicted from metagenomic screening)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — moderate producer (Tier 2)
Neurotransmitter relevance: None documented

Faecalibacterium prausnitzii

CYP450 orthologs: None documented
β-glucuronidase: No
Neurotransmitter relevance: Major butyrate producer (Tier 1, confirmed). Butyrate supports gut barrier integrity and modulates neuroinflammation via gut-brain axis. Anti-inflammatory marker species.
Key reference: Sokol et al. 2008

Lactobacillus rhamnosus

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2C19-like activity (Tier 3, predicted)
β-glucuronidase: No — some strains actually produce β-glucuronidase inhibitors
Neurotransmitter relevance: GABA production (Tier 1, confirmed in vitro). Modulates GABA-A receptor expression via vagus nerve.
Key reference: Bravo et al. 2011

Lactobacillus reuteri

CYP450 orthologs: None documented
β-glucuronidase: No
Neurotransmitter relevance: Histamine production (Tier 1). May modulate H1 receptor-mediated effects relevant to Mirtazapine.
Key reference: Thomas et al. 2012

Enterococcus faecalis

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2D6-like activity (Tier 2, ~35% identity from CYPED database)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — strong producer (Tier 1, confirmed)
Other: Tyrosine decarboxylase (produces tyramine — MAOI interaction risk)
Neurotransmitter relevance: Dopamine production from L-DOPA (Tier 1, confirmed)
Key reference: van Kessel et al. 2019; Maini Rekdal et al. 2019

Ruminococcus gnavus

CYP450 orthologs: CYP3A4-like activity (Tier 3, predicted)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — strong producer (Tier 1, confirmed)
Neurotransmitter relevance: Tryptamine production (Tier 2). Serotonin precursor pathway.
Key reference: Williams et al. 2014

Eubacterium rectale

CYP450 orthologs: None documented
β-glucuronidase: Low (Tier 3)
Neurotransmitter relevance: Major butyrate producer (Tier 1). Anti-inflammatory.
Key reference: Louis & Flint 2009

PROTEOBACTERIA PHYLUM
Escherichia coli (commensal strains)

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2D6-like reductase (Tier 1, confirmed — well-characterized in pharmacomicrobiomics literature), CYP2C19-like activity (Tier 2, ~40% identity)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — strong producer (Tier 1, confirmed. E. coli β-glucuronidase is the most studied bacterial GUS enzyme — crystal structure solved)
Other: Nitroreductases, acetyltransferases
Neurotransmitter relevance: Serotonin (5-HT) modulation — E. coli can deconjugate serotonin metabolites (Tier 2)
Key reference: Wallace et al. 2010 (GUS structure); Zimmermann et al. 2019

Klebsiella pneumoniae

CYP450 orthologs: CYP3A4-like oxidase (Tier 2, ~38% identity), CYP2B6-like activity (Tier 3, predicted)
β-glucuronidase: Yes — moderate producer (Tier 2)
Other: Broad xenobiotic metabolism capability
Neurotransmitter relevance: None documented
Key reference: Koppel et al. 2017

Pseudomonas aeruginosa

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2D6-like monooxygenase (Tier 2, ~43% identity from CYPED), CYP3A4-like activity (Tier 2)
β-glucuronidase: No
Other: Extensive oxidoreductase repertoire
Neurotransmitter relevance: None documented
Key reference: CYPED database; Bellamine et al. 2003

ACTINOBACTERIA PHYLUM
Bifidobacterium longum

CYP450 orthologs: None documented
β-glucuronidase: Minimal — some strains have low-activity GUS variants (Tier 2)
Neurotransmitter relevance: GABA production (Tier 1, confirmed). Tryptophan metabolism modulation (Tier 2). Anti-anxiety effects documented in animal models.
Key reference: Barrett et al. 2012; Savignac et al. 2014

Bifidobacterium adolescentis

CYP450 orthologs: None documented
β-glucuronidase: No
Neurotransmitter relevance: SCFA production (acetate) (Tier 1). Folate synthesis (Tier 1).
Key reference: Duranti et al. 2017

Eggerthella lenta

CYP450 orthologs: CYP2D6-like reductase (Tier 1, confirmed — documented to reduce the benzisoxazole ring in risperidone and inactivate digoxin via cardiac glycoside reductase)
β-glucuronidase: No
Other: Cardiac glycoside reductase (cgr operon). This is the best-documented example of a single gut bacterium inactivating a specific drug.
Neurotransmitter relevance: Dopamine metabolism — can dehydroxylate catechols (Tier 2)
Key reference: Haiser et al. 2013 (digoxin); Zimmermann et al. 2019

VERRUCOMICROBIA PHYLUM
Akkermansia muciniphila

CYP450 orthologs: None documented
β-glucuronidase: No
Neurotransmitter relevance: Mucin degradation supports gut barrier function. Indirectly modulates serotonin availability by maintaining enterochromaffin cell health (Tier 2). Low abundance is a dysbiosis marker.
Key reference: Everard et al. 2013; Plovier et al. 2017

RULES

Output ONLY valid JSON.
Use ONLY data from the knowledge base above. If a bacterium in the patient's profile is NOT in your knowledge base, include it in the microbiome_summary but do NOT fabricate enzyme data for it.
For each drug from Agent 1, check EVERY enzyme in its metabolizing_enzymes list against EVERY taxon in the patient's microbiome.
estimated_activity is a function of BOTH evidence tier AND abundance: Tier 1 + high abundance = high activity. Tier 3 + low abundance = low activity. Use judgment for combinations.
For beta_glucuronidase_risk: only flag at_risk=true if the drug has glucuronidation=true in Agent 1's output AND the patient has beta-glucuronidase producing taxa.
Sequence homology does NOT guarantee functional equivalence. Always note this limitation in evidence_basis for Tier 2 and 3 matches.
Do NOT rank drugs. Do NOT make recommendations. Just map the interactions.
